Two swaps that save the most

Buy cheese in blocks, not bags. Pre-shredded runs 20¢ to over $1 more per pound, and the potato starch and cellulose coating that keeps it from clumping also stops it melting smoothly. Costs you two minutes with a grater; saves money and melts better in every quesadilla on your plan.

Granola from the bulk bin, not the cereal aisle. Boxed granola is one of the worst value-per-calorie items in the store — often 200+ calories per ⅓ cup and priced like a snack food. The bin version is a fraction of the cost. Check the added sugar on the bin label and pick the lowest.

One thing that isn't groceries

PackIt Freezable lunch bag — about $20, Target or Amazon. The bag itself is lined with freezable gel, so there's no separate ice pack to forget. Without it, Wednesday and Friday default to the shelf-stable pack every week.

Where the researched picks came from

  • Peanut butter — America's Test Kitchen ranked 21 jars and the regular hydrogenated-oil styles beat the "natural" ones outright. The cheap jar won; you don't need to pay up here.
  • Greek yogurt — Fage Total tops Consumer Reports' plain Greek ratings and delivers 20g protein per 100 calories, the best ratio of any mainstream brand. Chobani is the value runner-up at 15g/90cal.
  • Whey — Garage Gym Reviews lab-tested 150+ powders with Ellipse Analytics; ON Gold Standard is their budget pick, NSF Certified for Sport, verified at 98% of claimed protein.
  • Marinara — Rao's won ATK's jarred-sauce test on whole tomatoes and no added sugar. A 24-jar consumer test found store brands surprisingly close, so take the cheap jar if Rao's isn't on sale.
  • Meat sticks — Chomps carries 10g protein per 33g stick against 8g in Country Archer's 28g.
  • Canned chicken — Kirkland took first in several blind tests for real chunks rather than shreds, around $2/can.
  • Trail mix — Consumer Reports: nuts and fruit are the nutrition; yogurt-coated pieces add hydrogenated oil and sugar, candy coating adds more, and savory mixes can carry serious sodium.
  • Bars — Quest leads on protein-plus-fiber and is stocked at Costco. Barebells wins nearly every taste test if chalkiness has put you off bars before.
